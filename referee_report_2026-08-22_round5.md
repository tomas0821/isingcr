## 2. Fresh re-read: new issues surviving adversarial verification

Ten candidate issues were generated from an independent whole-manuscript pass; six survived adversarial verification (2 MAJOR, 4 MINOR). Four did not survive and are not reported as findings: a claimed contradiction between two McNemar significance counts for the canton-level 2026 rerun, a claimed inconsistency around the GAM-proxy-risk cantons' "e.g." qualifier being treated as an exhaustive list, a claimed overclaim about the GAM field's urban-mechanism interpretation resting on an uncontrolled bivariate correlation, and a claimed mismatch between two forms of MIDEPLAN's institutional name. None of the four held up under adversarial re-checking (details available on request; omitted here per the "for the record, not findings" instruction governing this round's process).

### Major

**F1 — Abstract's and Conclusion's headline "uncertainty confined to a small, geographically identifiable set of fault-line distritos" claim drops a hedge the paper's own Limitations section applies to exactly those flagship cases.**
Abstract (lines 74-76): "the real 2026 map is a robustly determined equilibrium, with genuine uncertainty confined to a small, geographically identifiable set of fault-line distritos" — stated unhedged. The Conclusion (lines 1701-1704) echoes this near-verbatim, also unhedged at the point of statement, though the Discussion's own synthesis (lines 1535-1539) does carry a generic hedge ("distinct from whether any single covariate result survives further scrutiny"). But the Limitations section (the sole subsection of Discussion) explicitly names the two flagship "fault-line" distritos supporting this claim (Tabarcia, Monterrey, from the multistability check) plus the cascade test's largest case (Orosi) as sitting in cantons independently flagged as at-risk for GAM-proxy misclassification — and states this "undermines cross-year consistency as a way of distinguishing genuine dynamics from a static labeling artifact." The multistability subsection's own body text contains near-identical hedging language. Given how thoroughly hedged nearly every other claim in this manuscript is (including elsewhere in the same abstract), an unqualified statement of the paper's single most prominent closing claim — using the specific word "genuine," which the caveat directly problematizes — reads as an inconsistency rather than routine abstract-level compression.
*Recommendation:* Append a short qualifying clause to both the abstract and conclusion instances, e.g. "...though two of the flagship cases behind this claim cannot be fully separated from a static GAM-proxy labeling artifact (Section~\ref{sec:limitations})" — mirroring the hedge already present in the Discussion's version of the same sentence, so all three echoes of this claim carry consistent caveats.

**F2 — Abstract remains approximately 506 words, roughly double a typical Physica A abstract, after two documented trimming passes (round 2's initial ~400-word flag, round 4's N4 fix).**
Direct word count of the `\begin{abstract}...\end{abstract}` block is 506 words. Round 4 measured 553 words and classified this MAJOR under framing/scope, recommending "a second trim pass targeting the first-contribution portion." That pass evidently happened (506 < 553, and the three p-values N4 also flagged are now correctly relocated to their home section per the N4 verification above) — but the abstract is still carrying specific percentage-point figures, seed counts, and per-section caveats for essentially every result in the paper (canton ablation, historical comparison, 2018 energetics, finite-size scaling, distrito ablation, MIDEPLAN, GAM, and all four diagnostics), and at ~506 words is still roughly double a ~200-300 word norm. This is the same underlying defect on its third consecutive round of review (round 2 → round 4's N4 → this round), each pass reducing the length somewhat without resolving the core scope problem: the abstract is still trying to state a headline number from all five contributions rather than orienting a reader around the paper's core claims.
*Recommendation:* A structural rewrite rather than another incremental trim — state the two core contributions and their headline results in 2-3 sentences each, move remaining per-analysis figures (seed counts, individual p-values, granularity-specific percentages) into the body, and target the actual Physica A word limit from the journal's author guidelines directly rather than iterating by feel.

### Minor

**F3 — PPSD→Pueblo Soberano "widely reported at the time" claim (round 4's N2 fix) still carries no citation of any kind, academic or press.**
Lines 341-346, as fixed for N2, correctly add the "not aware of a peer-reviewed source" hedge — but "widely reported at the time" itself remains uncited. A grep for PPSD/Chaves/Rodrigo/succession-related citations near this passage confirms zero footnote, URL, or `\cite` of any kind backing the characterization; the nearby citations (`cascantematamoros2006`, `camachosanchez2025`, `chavarriamora2022`) cover general Costa Rican political-geography heterogeneity, not this specific succession claim. This underlies the non-circular political field used in Section 3.6/6.3 — a secondary, already-hedged, non-significant ($p=0.265$) result rather than the paper's central headline claim, which tempers but doesn't eliminate the gap.
*Recommendation:* Cite at least one contemporaneous news article discussing the PPSD/Pueblo Soberano relationship, or soften "widely reported" to something that doesn't imply a citable, verifiable source exists.

**F4 — Abstract embeds a literal internal LaTeX cross-reference to a body section number.**
Line 60: "...though its formal statistical significance ranges from clearly significant to non-significant depending on which reasonable test or binarization is used (full breakdown in Section~\ref{sec:ablation-distrito})..." This is the only internal section cross-reference inside the abstract; abstracts are frequently displayed independently of the compiled paper (indexing databases, search snippets), where a resolved section number is meaningless out of context. Standard Elsevier/Physica A house style discourages this.
*Recommendation:* Paraphrase in-line ("...detailed in the distrito-level ablation results below") or cut the parenthetical entirely.

**F5 — Section 3.10's heading ("Capital-region membership (GAM): the strongest result of this search, for 2026") is the only Results subsection heading that renders an evaluative verdict rather than a neutral noun phrase or question, breaking the register used by all other 26 headings.**
Confirmed against the full list of Results/Data subsection headings: every other heading is either a neutral noun phrase (e.g., "A second predisposition field: socioeconomic development") or a direct question (e.g., "Is there a critical point?", "Why is 2018 different?"). This heading's superlative is substantiated in the body (McNemar $p\approx0$, 15/16 seed-pairs significant, called "the most decisive result of any field tested in this paper" in the prose), so this is a register/consistency issue, not a factual one. Note this heading was itself the product of round-4's N3 fix, which correctly scoped the claim to "for 2026" but did not address the register break.
*Recommendation:* Retitle to match the collection's neutral convention, e.g. "Capital-region membership (GAM)," and let the body carry the superlative claim as it already does.

**F6 — "district-level" (English) appears 3 times (lines 185, 292, 1691) where the paper's established, dominant term (103 occurrences of "distrito," 18 of "distrito-level") is the untranslated Spanish "distrito."**
Line 292 is the most locally visible instance: it opens the MIDEPLAN subsection, whose own paragraph otherwise uses "distrito"/"distrito-level" repeatedly. Line 1691 (Conclusion) sits in a paragraph likewise otherwise using "distrito-level" freely. Line 185 is in the Introduction (not, as an earlier draft of this candidate finding mislabeled it, the abstract — the abstract ends at line 77). This is a genuine, narrow terminology leftover, not new-round-of-review noise; the paper conspicuously establishes "distrito" as its chosen technical term everywhere else, which is exactly why these three stand out.
*Recommendation:* Replace all three with "distrito-level" for consistency with the paper's own established convention.

## 3. Closing verdict

**Round-4 fix compliance: complete.** All 11 "must-fix" findings (N1-N11) verify as fixed against the current manuscript text, and the one deliberate non-fix (N12) was correctly left alone as documented. No fix was found to have overcorrected into a new contradiction, and the two places at highest risk of a botched follow-through — N4's abstract trim (checked to confirm the p-values actually landed in their home section rather than being silently dropped) and N5's restored transition (checked against the paragraph it now points back to) — both hold up under direct inspection.

**The manuscript is closer to ready than at any prior round, but not yet clean.** The fresh re-read surfaced 6 genuine new issues (2 MAJOR, 4 MINOR), down from round 4's 12 (5 MAJOR, 7 MINOR) — roughly half the volume, and the MAJOR count fell from 5 to 2. This is real evidence of convergence: four of round 4's five MAJOR findings (N1, N2, N3, N5) are now fully resolved with no fresh MAJOR issue arising in their place, and the fix pass this round did not introduce any new mechanical fallout of the kind that dominated round 4's list (N7, N8, N9, N11 there were all artifacts of round-3's own reorganization; this round's MINOR items are largely pre-existing gaps the earlier rounds' narrower scope hadn't reached, e.g. F5's register break and F6's terminology leftover, rather than fresh damage from this round's edits).

**The one place convergence is not happening is the abstract itself (F2).** This is the third consecutive round to flag its length as a MAJOR issue (round 2 informally, round 4 as N4, this round as F2), and each pass has trimmed it by a comparable, insufficient increment (round 4: 553 words; this round: 506) without addressing the structural problem — the abstract is still organized as a compressed results section rather than an abstract. Continuing to iterate on this specific paragraph with the same incremental-trim approach is unlikely to converge; it would benefit from a single deliberate rewrite pass against the target journal's actual word-count guideline rather than another round of referee-flagged shortening.

**Recommended path:** the two MAJOR items (F1, F2) should be fixed before this draft proceeds to novelty-check finalization or submission prep — F1 is a small, mechanical hedge addition; F2 needs the structural rewrite described above, not another trim. The four MINOR items (F3-F6) are all cheap, text-only corrections (a citation or a softened claim, a cut/paraphrased cross-reference, a heading rename, three word substitutions) and can be batched into the same pass. Given that the abstract-length issue has now survived three rounds of incremental fixing, a round-6 verification pass is warranted specifically to confirm F2 was resolved by a genuine rewrite rather than a fourth incremental trim — everything else in this round's findings looks like normal, converging tail-end polish.
