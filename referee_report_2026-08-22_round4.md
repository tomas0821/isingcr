# Round-4 Referee Verification Report

**Scope.** This round has two parts. First, it checks whether each of the 22 findings from the round-3 referee report (14 major, 8 minor) was actually fixed in the current draft — not whether the fix is the *best possible* fix, but whether the text now matches what the fix was supposed to accomplish, checked against the manuscript source, the underlying data files, and (where applicable) `00_Master_Notebook.md` and `NOVELTY_CHECK.md`. Second, it is a fresh, independent adversarial re-read of the same material, hunting specifically for defects the fix/editing pass itself might have introduced — new inconsistencies, stale cross-references, or framing that drifted out of alignment with the surrounding text during editing.

**Headline result.** All 22 round-3 findings verify as **fixed**: 0 partially fixed, 0 not fixed, 0 newly broken. This is a substantially cleaner outcome than round 3's own pass over round 2, and the fixes hold up under direct inspection of file content, on-disk data artifacts, and cross-references between sections — not just a re-reading of the specific sentence each finding originally flagged.

However, the fresh independent re-read is not clean. Of the candidate issues generated and adversarially checked, **12 survive verification** (5 major, 7 minor) as genuine, checkable defects — the great majority of them artifacts of the fix/reorganization pass itself (a moved paragraph left an orphaned transition; a merged subsection left a summary list in stale order; a renamed section broke a previously-consistent heading pattern; an arithmetic claim was generalized across two elections when it only holds for one). None of the 12 rises to the level of undermining a headline scientific claim, but two (the "more than double" domain-wall claim and the orphaned Discussion transition) are the kind of thing a Physica A referee would flag on a first read, and the abstract-length and GAM-framing items are the kind of thing an editor would ask to see addressed before proceeding.

## 2. Fresh re-read: new issues surviving adversarial verification

Twelve issues survived independent adversarial verification against the current manuscript text (five MAJOR, seven MINOR, all under the `consistency`, `overclaiming`, or `scope` lenses). Each was checked against the actual line numbers, cross-referenced against sibling sections, and in several cases against git history to confirm the defect is a genuine byproduct of the fix/editing pass rather than a pre-existing issue round 3 simply hadn't reached. They are presented here by severity, with a recommended fix for each.

### Major

**N1 — "More than double" boundary-vs-interior error claim is false for 2022, true only for 2026.**
Lines ~1289-1291: "boundary distritos show more than double the per-node error rate of interior ones: 38.0% versus 16.2% for 2026, 51.0% versus 29.7% for 2022." The 2026 ratio (38.0/16.2 = 2.35) supports "more than double"; the 2022 ratio (51.0/29.7 = 1.72) does not. The absolute point-gaps are similar (21.8 vs. 21.3 points), but "double" unambiguously claims a ratio, and the sentence states both elections' numbers as if both instantiate the same "more than double" claim. This is a straightforward arithmetic check any referee will run.
*Recommendation:* Replace the blanket ratio claim with language that separately and accurately characterizes each year, e.g. "more than double for 2026 (38.0% vs. 16.2%) and substantially higher, though short of double, for 2022 (51.0% vs. 29.7%, a 1.7x ratio)" — or drop the ratio framing entirely and lead with the point-gap, which is consistent across both years.

**N2 — PPSD→Pueblo Soberano succession claim is unsourced, despite being load-bearing for the non-circular political field's validity.**
Lines ~345-349: "the same Rodrigo Chaves-aligned movement under a different registered vehicle, confirmed via national press coverage, not merely a naming coincidence" — no citation, outlet, date, or footnote anywhere in the manuscript or `references.bib`. This breaks the paper's own established convention of citing sources for comparably-scoped political-geography claims (`\citep{cascantematamoros2006,camachosanchez2025,chavarriamora2022}` at line 156/876; a named decree number and Gaceta issue for the GAM boundary). The claim is the stated premise for treating this field as a "genuinely independent" (non-circular) predisposition proxy rather than an arbitrary covariate, though the section's r=0.70 empirical correlation provides some independent cushioning.
*Recommendation:* Add an actual citation (a specific news article/outlet and date, or a scholarly source discussing the PPSD/Pueblo Soberano relationship) or, if none is readily citable, soften "confirmed via national press coverage" to something checkable/hedged, e.g. "widely reported at the time as the same movement under a new registered name (though we are not aware of a peer-reviewed source confirming this)."

**N3 — "Strongest and most robust field found" / GAM subsection title overstate generality the paper's own numbers (and later text) contradict.**
Introduction (line ~194-195) and the GAM subsection title (line 1201, "Capital-region membership (GAM): the strongest result of this search") use unhedged "robust"/"strongest" language before any caveat appears. The paper's own numbers show p=0.0005 (15/16 seed-pairs) at 2026-distrito but only p=0.239 (4/16) at 2022-distrito, and 0/8 seed-pairs significant at canton level for any of the three years — and the Discussion itself, a few sentences after repeating "more robust," concedes "We do not extend this to a general claim... the result does not replicate for 2022," with the GAM subsection body separately calling it "2026-specific rather than a general property." The abstract, to its credit, states the caveat in the same sentence as the claim — but the Introduction and the subsection title do not.
*Recommendation:* Hedge the Introduction sentence and the subsection title to match the abstract's practice, e.g. "the strongest field found for 2026" or "strongest result of this search (2026-distrito only; see caveats in Section~\ref{sec:gam})," so a reader skimming only the Introduction isn't left with a stronger impression of generality than the McNemar numbers two sections later support.

**N4 — Abstract is ~553 words, roughly double Physica A's customary 200-300 word norm, and reads as a compressed results section.**
Direct word count (`\begin{abstract}...\end{abstract}`) confirms 553 words with no citation macros inflating the count. Individual sentences run to 100+ words with multiple nested parentheticals (e.g., the distrito-resolution reversal sentence carries four). This persists even after the M12 trim (which correctly right-sized the *second-contribution* portion to 142 words but left the first-contribution portion at ~411 words on its own). Note: one candidate elaboration of this finding claimed the abstract contains "Bonferroni-corrected figures" — checked and this is false (zero Bonferroni mentions inside the abstract's line range); the core word-count and density claim, however, is accurate and independently verified.
*Recommendation:* A second trim pass targeting the first-contribution portion specifically (which was flagged as an isolated ~400-word target in round 2 per the notes on M12, but never revisited on its own) is likely needed to bring the whole abstract within striking distance of journal norms — move per-test/per-blocking-granularity p-value breakdowns (e.g., the three-granularity median-p list) into the Results, keeping only the headline number and significance status in the abstract.

**N5 — Orphaned transition: "This reframes the earlier canton-level finding..." lost its antecedent when the Discussion was reordered.**
The Discussion's current order is: (1) resolution-dependence lesson, (2) absent-critical-point/FSS aside, (3) 2018-anomaly aside (ending on Moran's I vs. energetic favorability), (4) "This reframes the earlier canton-level finding..." — a sentence that only makes sense as a direct continuation of paragraph (1), not paragraph (3). Git history (commit 638a05b) confirms this paragraph was moved from directly after paragraph (1) to its current position, without the transition being rewritten. A reader arriving at paragraph (4) from paragraph (3) has a plausible-but-wrong local antecedent ("This" reading as pointing to the Moran's-I discussion) rather than the correct one.
*Recommendation:* Either move paragraph (4) back to sit immediately after paragraph (1), or rewrite its opening to name its antecedent explicitly, e.g. "Returning to the resolution-dependence point above, this reframes the earlier canton-level finding of Section~\ref{sec:ablation-canton}..."

### Minor

**N6 — Polarization-trend section cites only `sec:ablation-distrito` for a parenthetical that reports both the distrito (+8.7pp) and canton (+1.2pp) figures, but the canton figure's home section is `sec:ablation-canton`.**
Line ~1417-1419: "the same granularity pattern already documented for the own-margin field (Section~\ref{sec:ablation-distrito}: $+8.7$pp/significant at distrito versus $+1.2$pp/not-significant at canton)." The +1.2pp figure is established in `sec:ablation-canton` (line ~612-620: "a gap of $+1.2$ percentage points..."), not in `sec:ablation-distrito`. The manuscript's own convention elsewhere (lines 233-234, 442, 559, 1465, 1539, 1561) is to cite both sections jointly when referring to both figures, making this single citation an inconsistency relative to the paper's own established pattern.
*Recommendation:* Change to "Sections~\ref{sec:ablation-canton}/\ref{sec:ablation-distrito}" to match the paper's own citation convention elsewhere.

**N7 — "The crossing count remains 5" contradicts the "6 crossings" reported for the initial (light) run two paragraphs earlier.**
Lines ~822-823 report the initial 500/500-sweep run found 6 crossings; line ~838 describes the heavy re-run's result as "the crossing count remains 5." "Remains" lexically asserts no change from the previously-stated value, but 5 ≠ 6. The underlying numbers themselves appear correct (per `00_Master_Notebook.md`, light run = 6, heavy run = 5) — this is a word-choice defect, not a data error.
*Recommendation:* Replace "remains 5" with "is 5" or "changes only marginally, from 6 to 5."

**N8 — Introduction/Conclusion list the four model-native diagnostics in an order (domain-wall, multistability, counterfactual, cascade) that no longer matches the body's actual presentation order (multistability first, then the merged domain-wall/counterfactual/cascade subsection).**
Confirmed at Introduction line ~195-197 and near-identically in the Conclusion (~1687-1690). The body presents `sec:multistability` first (explicitly referenced by the merged subsection's opening line, "Section~\ref{sec:multistability}'s multistability check above"), and the Discussion's own walkthrough also opens with multistability. This reads as a stale artifact of moving the multistability subsection earlier without re-syncing the two roadmap sentences that summarize it.
*Recommendation:* Reorder the Introduction/Conclusion list to "multistability, domain-wall, counterfactual, cascade" (or whatever order best matches the final body/Discussion sequence) for consistency.

**N9 — "Temperature-fragile" is used as a load-bearing classification label in the cascade table and prose, but is never defined anywhere in the paper.**
Table~\ref{tab:cascade} labels three rows (Orosi, Piedras Negras, Hatillo) "temp-fragile," and the prose credits Orosi's larger cascade partly to being "additionally temperature-fragile" (line ~1342). The immediately preceding counterfactual-sensitivity paragraph reports only two scan-wide aggregate numbers (2.9% divergence at T=0.05, 10.9% at T=5.0), with no per-distrito breakdown or stated fragility criterion. By contrast, sibling categories in the same table are traceably defined: "multistable"/"seed-locked" trace to the explicit multistability score defined in Section~\ref{sec:multistability} (score=0 "fully locked," score≥4/16 "real multistability"); "boundary"/"periphery" trace to the domain-wall interface definition. "Temperature-fragile" has no equivalent definition.
*Recommendation:* Add one sentence in the counterfactual paragraph defining the per-distrito fragility criterion actually used to select these three rows (e.g., "flips sign at least once across the T-scan" or a specific divergence threshold), so the table's categorization is reproducible from the text.

**N10 — "Current-cycle-specific factors evidently matter more" attributes explanatory power to an entirely unmeasured causal category.**
Line ~1195-1199: "geography and current-cycle-specific factors evidently matter more here than raw historical partisan continuity," presented as "a reportable finding in its own right." No current-cycle-specific (2026-campaign-specific) covariate is ever operationalized or tested anywhere in the manuscript — the only three additional fields tested are MIDEPLAN (a slowly-varying 2023 index), the 2022-margin continuity field, and GAM (a static geographic boundary). The "geography" half of the sentence has independent support (the 67.64% geography-only baseline); the "current-cycle-specific factors" half is inferred purely by exclusion from the continuity field's weak performance, which is a residual/exclusionary inference dressed as a positive, "evidently" demonstrated result — a lapse from the manuscript's otherwise careful, hedge-heavy statistical culture.
*Recommendation:* Soften to something like "...suggesting factors other than raw historical partisan continuity — whether geographic or specific to this election cycle — play a larger role, though this study does not isolate or test any such cycle-specific covariate directly."

**N11 — Data section and Results section both use the byte-for-byte identical subsection title "A non-circular political field."**
Line 338 (`\label{sec:data-political}`) and line 1169 (`\label{sec:noncircular}`) carry the exact same heading text, unlike the parallel MIDEPLAN pair ("Socioeconomic development (MIDEPLAN IDS 2023)" vs. "A second predisposition field: socioeconomic development") and GAM pair ("Gran Área Metropolitana (GAM) membership" vs. "Capital-region membership (GAM): the strongest result of this search"), both of which deliberately differentiate Data-section from Results-section headings. This will render as duplicate ToC/PDF-bookmark entries and reads as an oversight from whatever pass renamed the other two pairs.
*Recommendation:* Rename the Results-section heading to something differentiated, e.g. "A second predisposition field: political continuity" or "Testing the non-circular political field," matching the pattern used for MIDEPLAN and GAM.

**N12 — Title's trailing "with a Search for Non-Circular Predisposition Fields" clause structurally subordinates a contribution that occupies close to half the Results and Discussion content.**
Of 13 Results subsections, 7 belong to the second contribution (44% by word count: ~2863 of ~6477 words); the Discussion (excluding Limitations) splits almost exactly 50/50 between the two contributions (467 vs. 457 words); combined Discussion+Limitations splits ~55/45%. The one place this "roughly half" characterization does not hold is the abstract itself, where the second contribution occupies only ~26% (142 of ~553 words) — so the title's subordination is arguably more consistent with the abstract's own emphasis than with the body's. This is a genuine but low-stakes framing tension, not a factual error.
*Recommendation:* Optional. If the authors want the title to better reflect the body's balance, consider promoting the second contribution to co-equal billing (e.g., "...and a Search for Non-Circular Predisposition Fields" as a second main clause rather than a trailing "with" clause) — but this is a judgment call, not a correctness issue, and reasonable authors could leave it as is.

## 3. Closing verdict

**Round-3 fix compliance: complete.** All 14 major and 8 minor round-3 findings verify as fixed against the current manuscript text, the underlying data artifacts, and the project's tracking documents (`00_Master_Notebook.md`, `NOVELTY_CHECK.md`). No fix was found to have overcorrected into a new contradiction, and the two places most at risk of a botched merge/reorder (MIN7's Discussion regrouping, M14's subsection merge) both hold up under direct inspection, including a full document-wide `\ref`/`\label` cross-check that found zero undefined or duplicate references.

**But the manuscript is not yet ready to move forward as-is.** The fresh re-read surfaced 12 genuine new issues, 5 of them MAJOR:

- Two (N1, N5) are outright internal inconsistencies a referee will catch on a normal read: a ratio claim that's arithmetically false for one of the two years it's applied to, and a paragraph whose opening pronoun no longer has a valid antecedent after a Discussion reorder.
- One (N2) is a sourcing gap on a claim central to motivating a named robustness check, out of step with the paper's own citation practice elsewhere.
- Two (N3, N4) are framing/scope issues — generality-implying language that the paper's own later numbers walk back, and an abstract still roughly double the target journal's customary length even after the M12 trim addressed only half the problem.

None of these threatens the paper's central scientific claims, and several (N1, N5, N7, N8) are purely mechanical fallout from the editing/reorganization passes that fixed the round-3 findings — the kind of thing that surfaces specifically *because* a manuscript was heavily edited, not because the underlying physics or statistics changed. That said, five MAJOR-severity items is enough that this round should not be treated as a clean pass.

**Recommended path:** one more fix pass targeting N1-N5 (the MAJOR items) is warranted before this draft proceeds to novelty-check finalization or submission prep; the seven MINOR items (N6-N12) can be batched into the same pass cheaply, since none requires new data or analysis — all are text-only corrections (a citation, a word substitution, a heading rename, a reordered list, one added definition sentence). Given the pattern in this project's own history (round 3 catching things round 2 missed, and this round catching things introduced by round 3's own fix pass), a round-5 verification pass specifically re-checking N1-N5 before submission is advisable rather than assuming a single additional edit pass is self-verifying.
